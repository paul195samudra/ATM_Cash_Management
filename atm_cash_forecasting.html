Why this exists

Five outcomes the forecast has to earn.

No shortages

An empty ATM is a broken promise to a customer. The forecast exists to make that a rare event, not a routine one.

No idle cash

Cash sitting unused in a vault is a cost, not a safety margin. Over-provisioning is failure dressed as caution.

Optimised schedules

Replenishment should follow demand, not a fixed calendar. The model decides the rhythm, not the roster.

Lower transport cost

Every cash-in-transit run has a cost. Fewer, better-timed runs beat frequent, defensive ones.

Better customer experience

The measure that matters most sits outside the model — a working machine when someone needs it.

What goes into the model

Six ledgers of information, lettered the way the brief tracks them.

Historical cash demand carries the highest priority. Yesterday's withdrawal and the last 3 days explain more of tomorrow's number than almost anything else in the dataset — every other category refines that baseline rather than replacing it.

Time features answer a calendar question. Day of week, week number, month, quarter, weekends, public holidays, festivals, salary day, and start/end-of-month flags exist to test two things: do Fridays always run higher, and does demand climb before a holiday.

ATM behaviour features describe the machine itself. Average daily withdrawal, transaction count, peak hour, average transaction size, daily max/min, and cash utilisation rate separate the busy machines from the stable ones from the volatile ones.

Location features describe the neighbourhood. Mall, residential, business district, university, airport, station, tourist area, rural vs urban — each setting has its own weekly shape, like mall ATMs peaking on weekends and office ATMs peaking on weekdays.

Refill behaviour features turn a forecast into a task. Last refill date, days since refill, previous refill amount, current balance, ATM capacity, and percentage of cash remaining are what convert a demand number into a replenish decision.

External factors are optional by design. Temperature, rainfall, public events, sports matches, festivals, and elections only earn a place in the model if they measurably improve the forecast — they're tested, not assumed.

Operational constraints the plan has to respect

Physical

Staff and vehicle availability, cash-collection SLAs, and storage-point capacity all cap how fast a refill can actually happen.

Financial

Insurance, CIT service cost, cash rent, and bank fees mean the cheapest schedule isn't always the most frequent one.

Logical

Routes should avoid cross-deliveries of the same denominations, and account for real travel time between points.

Regulatory

National regulator rules and the cash centre's internal policy set hard floors and ceilings the model can't override.
